How edibles can help with pain

By HowToEdibles Editorial Team March 5, 2026 7 min read

Cannabis edibles offer deep, long-lasting pain relief for chronic conditions, inflammation, and nerve damage — lasting 4 to 8 hours from a single dose.

Home/Articles/How edibles can help with pain

Short answer

Edibles can help some people with chronic pain, but the benefit is usually modest. The National Academies (2017) found conclusive or substantial evidence that cannabis is effective for chronic pain in adults, and NIH's NCCIH describes the benefit in later reviews as small. Edibles take 30 minutes to 2 hours to start working and last several hours, so start with about 2.5–5 mg THC and wait at least 2 hours before taking more.

Not medical advice

This article is general education, not medical advice. Talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using cannabis for pain, especially if you take opioids, sedatives, blood thinners or other prescription drugs, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have a history of mental illness or substance use.

Pain is the number one reason people turn to medical cannabis: chronic pain is the most common qualifying condition reported by U.S. medical cannabis patients, accounting for roughly two-thirds of them in 2016. Edibles are a popular way to use it for pain because a single dose lasts much longer than smoking. Whether you are dealing with chronic back pain, arthritis, fibromyalgia, migraines, or nerve damage, cannabis edibles offer a unique combination of benefits that other pain relief methods struggle to match.

Why edibles work so well for pain

When you eat cannabis, it passes through your digestive system and is metabolized by the liver. The liver converts delta-9-THC into 11-hydroxy-THC, an active metabolite. After an edible, blood levels of 11-hydroxy-THC are about as high as THC itself, while after smoking they reach only about a tenth of THC levels. That's one reason edible effects often feel stronger and last much longer than smoking the same amount.

While smoking provides relief that lasts 1–3 hours, a single dose of edibles can provide pain relief for 4 to 8 hours. For people with chronic pain, this means fewer doses throughout the day and more consistent relief, especially overnight when pain often disrupts sleep.

Types of pain edibles can help with

Inflammatory pain

Conditions like arthritis, Crohn's disease, and general joint inflammation are common reasons people try cannabis, because THC and CBD show anti-inflammatory effects in laboratory and animal studies, partly through CB2 receptors on immune cells. Human evidence is weaker: a Cochrane review concluded that the effects of cannabis on Crohn's disease "are uncertain," and surveys of arthritis patients who use CBD report less pain but can't rule out a placebo effect.

Neuropathic pain

Nerve pain — caused by conditions like diabetes, multiple sclerosis, or injury — is notoriously difficult to treat with conventional painkillers. Neuropathic pain is one of the better-studied uses of cannabinoids. NCCIH notes that a 2018 review of 16 trials, most testing nabiximols (a THC/CBD mouth spray), found low- to moderate-quality evidence of better pain relief than placebo.

Muscle pain and spasms

Cannabis acts as a muscle relaxant, making it useful for conditions that involve chronic tension or spasticity. The strongest evidence here is for multiple sclerosis: the National Academies found conclusive or substantial evidence that oral cannabinoids improve patient-reported MS spasticity. Many people with back pain or fibromyalgia also report that edibles help relax tight muscles.

Headaches and migraines

While research is still emerging, many migraine sufferers report significant relief from cannabis edibles. The combination of pain reduction, anti-inflammatory action, and anti-nausea effects makes edibles a comprehensive tool for managing severe headaches.

Dosing edibles for pain relief

Finding the right dose is critical. Too little and you will not get adequate relief; too much and you may experience unwanted side effects like anxiety, dizziness or excessive sedation. There is no established cannabis dose for any pain condition, so the table below describes typical effects by dose for adults, not a treatment plan:

Dose level THC amount Typical effect
Microdose 1–2.5 mg Subtle or no noticeable high; a cautious first dose for new, older or sensitive users
Low dose 2.5–5 mg The usual starting range; mild effects for most people
Standard dose 5–15 mg Clear intoxication and impairment; only once you know how you respond
High dose 15–30 mg Strong impairment and higher risk of anxiety; experienced users only

Start at the low end, wait at least 2 hours before taking more, and increase in small steps on separate days. Don't drive after taking an edible. If you take prescription medication, check with a doctor or pharmacist first: the FDA warns that CBD can cause liver injury and change how other drugs work, and combining cannabis with opioids, benzodiazepines, sleep medicines or alcohol increases sedation.

THC vs CBD for pain

Both cannabinoids have pain-relieving properties, but they work differently. THC directly alters pain perception in the brain, making you feel less bothered by pain even when it is still present. CBD does not produce a high and has anti-inflammatory effects in laboratory studies, but its pain-relieving effect in people is less well established.

THC:CBD ratios for pain

For pain management, many people find that a combination of THC and CBD works better than either compound alone. A ratio of 1:1 THC:CBD is a popular choice, and much of the trial evidence for neuropathic pain comes from nabiximols, a spray with roughly equal THC and CBD. No ratio has been proven best, though.

Edibles vs prescription painkillers

Cannabis edibles are not a replacement for professional medical advice, but they offer several advantages over conventional painkillers. Unlike opioids, cannabis carries virtually no risk of fatal overdose. It can still be habit-forming, though: the CDC estimates that about 3 in 10 people who use cannabis have cannabis use disorder. Unlike NSAIDs, long-term cannabis use does not cause stomach ulcers or kidney damage. And unlike many prescription medications, edibles are something you can make yourself at home from a plant.

If you are currently taking prescription pain medication, talk to your doctor before adding cannabis to your routine — there can be interactions, and your dosages may need adjustment.
